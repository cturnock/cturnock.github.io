Getting the footage the camera decided not to keep

Cameras only saved clips when a sensor tripped. What fleet managers needed was almost always in the part that got discarded — one vehicle, one afternoon, usually because a customer had called. The requirement I was handed couldn't deliver that, I said so, built it anyway, and watched a VP stop it. That's how I got the room to solve it properly.

Concept · self-directed vision work

Fleet directors don't struggle to find data. They struggle to know what to prioritize.

The product was organized around the data we happened to store — fuel, safety, maintenance — and no director is measured on any of those individually. So I reorganized the concept around the goals they're accountable for, and kept the reasoning inspectable underneath every recommendation.

About

I've been in GM's commercial and fleet space for nearly four years — about two and a half years longer than anyone else on my team. In practice that makes me the institutional memory: people ask me where something lives and why it was decided that way, including designers a level above me.

Most of what I work on starts ambiguous or already broken. The design is usually the last third of the job. The first two thirds are getting several teams to the same version of the problem, then keeping the answer intact while product, engineering, legal, and leadership argue over it.

Before GM I was the only full-time designer at Asure Software, building a green-field product while maintaining the legacy ones. Before that, UX and UI at Brightly Interactive.

Outside of work I'm in Grand Rapids, Michigan, mostly organized around my two kids — tinkering in the garage, running a D&D campaign, or working through whatever the house needs next.
